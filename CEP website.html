Types of Waste

Understanding how to categorize waste is the first step toward effective recycling and disposal[cite: 1].

Wet Waste

Organic kitchen waste, food scraps, vegetable peels, and garden waste that can be easily composted.

Dry Waste

Recyclable materials including paper, cardboard, plastics, glass, metals, and packaging materials.

E-Waste

Discarded electronic devices such as old computers, mobile phones, batteries, and television sets.

Hazardous Waste

Toxic materials including chemicals, paints, medical waste, and fluorescent bulbs that require special handling.

Waste Management Tips

Adopt these core practices to minimize your daily environmental footprint[cite: 1]:

  • Reduce: Minimize consumption by purchasing only what you need and avoiding single-use items[cite: 1].
  • Reuse: Find alternative creative uses for items before throwing them away[cite: 1].
  • Recycle: Sort your dry recyclables correctly so they can be processed into new goods[cite: 1].
  • Composting: Turn your kitchen scraps and wet waste into nutrient-rich soil for plants[cite: 1].
  • Plastic Reduction: Switch to reusable cloth bags, steel water bottles, and glass containers[cite: 1].
